# Healthcare Analytics Project

---

## Project Overview

Healthcare organizations generate large volumes of patient, admission, and diagnosis data that can be used to improve operational efficiency and patient outcomes.

This project analyzes deidentified clinical data from the **MIMIC-IV Demo** dataset using **PostgreSQL**, **Python**, and **Power BI** to evaluate hospital operations, patient demographics, diagnosis patterns, length of stay, and in-hospital mortality.

The project demonstrates an end-to-end analytics workflow beginning with data preparation in PostgreSQL, exploratory analysis and statistical testing in Python, and interactive dashboard development in Power BI.

PostgreSQL was used to store, validate, join, and transform the data into analytical views. Python was used for exploratory data analysis, visualization, and statistical testing. Power BI was used to create an interactive dashboard for communicating hospital performance and patient outcome metrics.

---

## Business Questions

The project examines the following questions:

* How many patients and hospital admissions are represented?
* What is the average hospital length of stay?
* Which admission types have the highest patient volume?
* Which patient groups experience longer hospital stays?
* What are the most common diagnoses?
* Which diagnoses are associated with longer stays or higher mortality?
* How does mortality vary across age groups, admission types, and insurance categories?
* Are differences in length of stay statistically significant?

# Key Findings

The analysis identified several important patterns within the dataset:

- **275 hospital admissions** were generated by only **100 unique patients**, indicating repeated hospital utilization.
- **Emergency admissions** represented the largest portion of hospital activity, accounting for approximately **38%** of all admissions.
- Patients remained hospitalized for an average of **6.9 days**, with the average and median LOS being nearly identical, suggesting relatively consistent hospitalization durations.
- Patients aged **65 years and older** experienced the highest mortality rate (approximately **8.7%**).
- **Urgent admissions** had the highest mortality among admission categories (approximately **13.2%**).
- Male patients experienced a higher mortality rate than female patients despite a nearly balanced gender distribution.
- Acute kidney failure was among the most frequently occurring primary diagnoses.
- Several diagnoses required substantially longer hospital stays than the hospital average, indicating greater resource utilization.

---

# Business Recommendations

Based on the analysis, several opportunities for improving hospital operations were identified.

### Improve discharge planning

Patients remained hospitalized for an average of nearly **7 days**, with several diagnoses requiring substantially longer stays. Improving discharge coordination and post-acute care planning for these conditions may reduce unnecessary bed occupancy.

### Focus on high-risk patient populations

Patients aged **65+** and those admitted through **Urgent** admissions experienced the highest mortality rates. Earlier clinical interventions and closer monitoring for these patient groups may improve outcomes.

### Analyze repeat hospital utilization

The dataset contains **275 admissions for only 100 patients**, suggesting many patients returned for additional hospital care. A dedicated readmission analysis could identify opportunities to improve follow-up care and reduce avoidable readmissions.

### Optimize emergency department resources

Emergency-related admissions represented the largest share of hospital activity. Reviewing staffing models, bed utilization, and emergency department workflows may improve patient flow during periods of high demand.

### Review high-resource diagnoses

Several diagnoses were associated with significantly longer hospital stays than the overall average. Reviewing clinical pathways for these conditions may improve treatment efficiency while maintaining quality of care.
